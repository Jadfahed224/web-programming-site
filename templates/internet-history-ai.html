History of the Internet

Introduction

The internet is the global network infrastructure connecting a large number of computers and devices around the world.[cite: 1]

Key Milestones

  • 1969 - ARPANET becomes operational: ARPANET connected its first four nodes in the United States.[cite: 1] It demonstrated computer communication over a packet-switched network and became a major foundation of the Internet.[cite: 1]
  • 1973 - TCP/IP development begins: Robert Kahn and Vint Cerf began developing TCP/IP.[cite: 1] It provided a way for different networks to communicate and created the basis for an interconnected network of networks.[cite: 1]
  • 1983 - ARPANET switches to TCP/IP: ARPANET adopted TCP/IP as its standard protocol suite.[cite: 1] This allowed different networks to communicate using a common system and was an important step toward the modern Internet.[cite: 1]
  • 1986 - NSFNET is launched: The National Science Foundation launched NSFNET to connect universities and research centers.[cite: 1] It became an important backbone for the growing Internet in the United States.[cite: 1]
  • 1990 - ARPANET is decommissioned: ARPANET was shut down as the larger Internet had grown beyond it, with TCP/IP becoming the dominant networking technology.[cite: 1]
  • 1995 - NSFNET backbone is decommissioned: The NSFNET backbone was shut down as private network providers expanded.[cite: 1] This marked the transition toward a commercially operated Internet.[cite: 1]

3 Key Internet Technologies

Packet Switching
Divides data into small packets that travel through a network and are reassembled at the destination.[cite: 1] It was fundamental to ARPANET.[cite: 1]
TCP/IP
A group of protocols that allows different networks to communicate.[cite: 1] IP handles addressing and routing, while TCP helps provide reliable data delivery.[cite: 1]
DNS (Domain Name System)
Converts human-readable domain names into IP addresses, making Internet resources easier to access.[cite: 1]
