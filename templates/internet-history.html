History of the Internet

Introduction

The internet is the global infrastructure connecting a large number of computers and devices around the world.

Key Milestones

  • 1969 - ARPANET becomes operational:

    ARPANET connected its first four nodes in the United States. It demonstrated computer communication over a packet-switched network and became a major foundation of the Internet.

    • Source: DARPA - ARPANET

  • 1973 - TCP/IP development begins:

    Robert Kahn and Vint Cerf began developing TCP/IP. It provided a way for different networks to communicate and created the basis for an interconnected network of networks.

    • Source: DARPA - TCP/IP

  • 1983 - ARPANET switches to TCP/IP:

    ARPANET adopted TCP/IP as its standard protocol suite. This allowed different networks to communicate using a common system and was an important step toward the modern Internet.

    • Source: DARPA - ARPANET

  • 1986 - NSFNET is launched:

    The National Science Foundation launched NSFNET to connect universities and research centers. It became an important backbone for the growing Internet in the United States.

    • Source: NSF - Birth of the Commercial Internet

  • 1990 - ARPANET is decommissioned:

    ARPANET was shut down as the larger Internet had grown beyond it, with TCP/IP becoming the dominant networking technology.

    • Source: DARPA - ARPANET

  • 1995 - NSFNET backbone is decommissioned:

    The NSFNET backbone was shut down as private network providers expanded. This marked the transition toward a commercially operated Internet.

    • Source: NSF - Birth of the Commercial Internet

  • 1997 - Wi-Fi is standardized:

    IEEE 802.11 was published, establishing a standard for wireless local-area networking and forming the basis for Wi-Fi networks.

    • Source: IEEE - IEEE 802.11-1997

  • 1998 - ICANN is founded:

    ICANN was created to coordinate Internet domain names and other unique Internet identifiers.

    • Source: ICANN - History of ICANN

  • 1999 - ADSL broadband is standardized:

    The ITU approved the ADSL standard, enabling higher-speed Internet access over existing telephone lines and supporting the growth of broadband Internet access.

    • Source: ITU - Recommendation G.992.1 (ADSL)

  • 2010 - 4G is officially recognized:

    The ITU designated LTE-Advanced and WirelessMAN-Advanced as IMT-Advanced technologies, qualifying them as true 4G technologies.

    • Source: ITU - IMT-Advanced 4G standards

  • 2012 - World IPv6 Launch begins:

    Major websites, Internet service providers, and equipment manufacturers permanently enabled IPv6, marking a major step in the transition from IPv4.

    • Source: Internet Society - World IPv6 Launch

  • 2016 - IANA stewardship transitions:

    The coordination and management of the Internet's unique identifiers transitioned from the U.S. government contract to the global Internet community.

    • Source: ICANN - IANA Stewardship Transition

  • 2021 - 5G specifications are published:

    The ITU published the IMT-2020 specifications, establishing the international technical framework for 5G mobile networks.

    • Source: ITU - IMT-2020 (5G)

  • 2026 - 6G requirements are established:

    ITU experts agreed on the performance requirements for IMT-2030, marking a major step toward the next generation of mobile connectivity.

    • Source: ITU - IMT-2030: Technical requirements for the 6G future

3 Key Technologies

  1. Switching:

    Divides data into small packets that travel through a network and are reassembled at the destination. It was fundamental to ARPANET.

  2. TCP/IP:

    A group of protocols that allows different networks to communicate. IP handles addressing and routing, while TCP helps provide reliable data delivery.

  3. DNS (Domain Name System):

    Converts human-readable domain names into IP addresses, making Internet resources easier to access.
